Data. Binding energies per nucleon are computed from measured nuclear masses (AME-standard values). Stage temperatures and durations are standard published values for a 1 M☉ and a 25 M☉ star; exact figures shift with mass and metallicity, and models differ by factors of a few for the late stages. The photon diffusion time is Mitalas & Sills (1992), ApJ: a 0.090 cm mean step gives ~170,000 yr for the present Sun. Defensible estimates run from tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand years depending on the assumed density profile.

Stylizations. Shells light up one at a time for clarity — in a massive star's final day all of them burn at once, in nested shells. Shell radii are schematic; at true scale the iron core would be an invisible dot. The chamber shows one representative reaction per stage out of a network of hundreds. The Sun's radiative and convective zones are drawn as they are today, during hydrogen burning; the layered core structure develops much later. A photon does not keep its identity for 170,000 years and then emerge as yellow sunlight — energy is absorbed and re-radiated countless times on the way out. 62Ni actually edges out 56Fe on binding energy per nucleon (8.795 vs 8.790 MeV); iron wins in stars because silicon burning runs through 56Ni, which decays to 56Fe. The shock-revival mechanism in the collapse animation is simplified and remains an open research question.
