A westerly wind burst is any spell of anomalous west-to-east wind over the warm pool — most often the Madden–Julian Oscillation passing through. Pick how strong, and watch what the ocean does with it. Not every burst produces an El Niño.

Case Study · Observed Event
The 2026–27 El Niño

Every westerly wind burst carries a pair of counter-rotating swirls, one either side of the equator — that is simply how the tropical atmosphere answers heating on the line. Usually they stay weak and unnamed. In early April 2026 they did not: they deepened into Super Typhoon Sinlaku north of the equator and Cyclone Maila south of it, and the westerlies trapped between them became one of the strongest bursts on record.
