Case Study · Observed Event
The 2026–27 El Niño

In early April 2026, two tropical cyclones spun up at almost the same longitude on opposite sides of the equator — Super Typhoon Sinlaku to the north and Cyclone Maila to the south. Because storms turn counter-clockwise north of the equator and clockwise south of it, the winds caught between the pair both blew the same way: from the west. That handoff produced one of the strongest westerly wind bursts on record and launched the downwelling Kelvin wave now crossing the Pacific. Press Play to run the real sequence.

● Neutral State
Neutral Conditions
Trade winds blow steadily westward along the equatorial Pacific, piling warm water into the western Pacific warm pool. The thermocline — the boundary between warm surface water and cold deep water — tilts steeply: roughly 150 m deep in the west, only 50 m in the east. Cold water upwells naturally along the coast of South America, keeping that region cool.
