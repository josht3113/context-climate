Data — Planet distances: Solar System Objects Data Table, NYS Earth & Space Sciences Reference Tables (2026 Rev. Ed.), p. 2. Earth layer depths and densities: Generalized Model of Earth’s Interior Structure, ESSRT (2026), p. 11. Planet densities: NASA NSSDCA Planetary Fact Sheet (Williams, 2025); Ceres: Park et al. (2016). Condensation temperatures (50% condensed, 10⁻⁴ bar): Wood et al. (2019), Am. Mineral. 104. ²⁶Al: half-life 0.717 Myr, heating 0.355 W per kg ²⁶Al (Castillo-Rogez et al., 2009). Melting thresholds, Al content, ²⁶Al/²⁷Al, thermal properties, 40% melt for core formation: Moskovitz & Gaidos (2011). Planet masses, inclinations: NASA NSSDCA. Accretion anchors: Jupiter core ~20 M⊕ by <1 Myr and ~50 M⊕ by 3–4 Myr (Kruijer et al., 2017); Mars at about half its mass by ~2 Myr (Dauphas & Pourmand, 2011); nebular gas gone by ~4 Myr (Wang et al., 2017); Moon-forming impact ~50 Myr (Thiemens et al., 2019) to 95 ± 32 Myr (Jacobson et al., 2014).

Modeled, not measured — Disk: steady viscous α-disk (α = 0.01) with midplane heating after Nakamoto & Nakagawa (1994), stellar irradiation T = 280 K·(r/AU)−½ (Hayashi, 1981), accretion rate 10⁻⁸ M☉/yr·(t/1 Myr)−1.5 (Hartmann et al., 1998), opacity 5·min[1, (T/170 K)²] cm²/g (after Mori et al., 2025). Solid mass shares are illustrative. Planet: peak-temperature energy balance, not a thermal-evolution simulation — ²⁶Al heat retained in proportion to conductive cooling time R²/(π²κ); 20% of accretion energy retained (assumed); core-formation heat from the change in gravitational energy. Layer sizes are uncompressed; crust thickness exaggerated. Accretion: a choreographed sequence on those anchors, not an N-body simulation — embryo count, merger order and dates, Saturn/Uranus/Neptune growth curves, and planetesimal clearing are illustrative; distance is log-scaled; giant-planet migration is not shown.
