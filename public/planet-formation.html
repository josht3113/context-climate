Planet Formation

Sources

Data

  • Planet distances, diameters and eccentricities: Solar System Objects Data Table, NYS Earth & Space Sciences Reference Tables (2026 Rev. Ed.), p. 2.
  • Earth's layers, depths and densities: Generalized Model of Earth's Interior Structure, ESSRT (2026), p. 11.
  • Mean densities, masses, inclinations: NASA NSSDCA Planetary Fact Sheet. Vesta: Russell et al. (2012), Science 336.
  • Condensation temperatures (50% condensed, 10⁻⁴ bar): Wood et al. (2019), Am. Mineral. 104 — Fe–Ni metal 1,338 K, silicate rock 1,343 K, water ice 183 K.
  • Mercury: core radius ≈ 2,020 km, outer shell density ≈ 3.38 g/cm³, crust 17–53 km — Hauck et al. (2013); Padovan et al. (2015); review by Margot et al. (2018). A solid FeS layer at the top of the core has been proposed (0–90 km) but is not drawn.
  • Venus: no seismic or tidal measurement of its core; a ~3,000 km iron core is assumed by analogy with Earth.
  • Moon: inner core 240 km, core 330 km, partly molten layer to 480 km radius, outer-core density ≈ 5 g/cm³ (Weber et al., 2011, Science 331, Apollo seismic data). Crust 34–43 km at 2.55 g/cm³ (Wieczorek et al., 2013, GRAIL).
  • Mars: core 1,650 ± 20 km at ≈ 6.5 g/cm³ with a ~150 km molten silicate layer above it (Samuel et al., 2023; Khan et al., 2023, Nature, InSight). Crust 42–56 km (Kim et al., 2023).
  • Vesta: core radius 107–113 km (Russell et al., 2012, Dawn). Crust thickness is an estimate.
  • Jupiter: dilute core reaching ~60% of the radius (Militzer et al., 2022; Militzer & Hubbard, 2023); molecular-to-metallic hydrogen transition near 1–2 million bar (Militzer & Hubbard, 2023), Juno gravity data.
  • Neptune: H–He–methane envelope to ~0.7–0.75 of the radius, water–ammonia–methane fluid mantle, rocky core poorly constrained (Helled et al., 2011; Scheibe et al., 2021), Voyager 2 gravity data.
  • Accretion anchors: Jupiter core ~20 Earth masses by <1 Myr and ~50 by 3–4 Myr (Kruijer et al., 2017); Mars at about half its mass by ~2 Myr (Dauphas & Pourmand, 2011); nebular gas gone by ~4 Myr (Wang et al., 2017); Moon-forming impact ~50 Myr (Thiemens et al., 2019) to 95 ± 32 Myr (Jacobson et al., 2014).

Modeled, not measured

  • Disk temperature: steady viscous α-disk (α = 0.01) with midplane heating after Nakamoto & Nakagawa (1994), stellar irradiation T = 280 K·(r/AU)−½ (Hayashi, 1981), accretion rate 10⁻⁸ M☉/yr·(t/1 Myr)−1.5 (Hartmann et al., 1998), opacity 5·min[1, (T/170 K)²] cm²/g.
  • Solid material: minimum-mass solar nebula, 7.1·(r/AU)−1.5 g/cm² of rock and metal, 30·(r/AU)−1.5 g/cm² once water ice condenses (Hayashi, 1981). Metal : rock = 32.5 : 67.5 by mass. Grain close-ups are illustrations.
  • Accretion: a choreographed sequence built on the anchors above, not an N-body simulation. Embryo count, merger order and dates, the growth of Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, and planetesimal clearing are illustrative. Giant-planet migration is not shown. In the Whole view distance is log-scaled; the Inner view is true scale.
  • Differentiation: the melting and sinking sequence is illustrative and ends at each body's measured or inferred layers. Crusts are drawn thicker than true scale. Jupiter's density follows an n = 1 polytrope (central pressure ≈ 40 million bar). Neptune's layer densities are chosen to match its mean density and are model-dependent. Venus's core is assumed.
