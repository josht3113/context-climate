The iron core has no fuel left that releases energy. Worse — the photons in it are now hot enough to take the core apart.

Result

Neutron star

This core settles into a neutron star roughly 20 km across. Progenitors above about 20–25 solar masses collapse further into a black hole. Everything above iron on the periodic table was made in the explosion and in the neutron-rich debris, not in the furnace.

Data. Binding energies per nucleon are computed from measured nuclear masses (AME-standard values). Stage temperatures and durations are the standard 25 M☉ set; exact values shift with the star's mass, and published models differ by factors of a few for the late stages. Coulomb barrier heights are computed as Z1Z2e²/4πε0R with R = 1.44(A11/3 + A21/3) fm; thermal energy is kT at the stage temperature.

Stylizations. Shells light up one at a time here for clarity — by the star's final day all of them are burning at once, in nested shells. Shell radii are schematic; at true scale the iron core would be an invisible dot. The chamber shows one representative reaction per stage out of a network of hundreds. 62Ni actually edges out 56Fe on binding energy per nucleon (8.795 vs 8.790 MeV) — iron wins in stars because silicon burning runs through 56Ni, which decays to 56Fe. The shock-revival mechanism in the collapse animation is simplified and is still an open research question.
