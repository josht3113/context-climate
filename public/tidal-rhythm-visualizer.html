Gravity follows the inverse square law (1/r²) — by that law alone, the Sun's gravity on Earth is about 180× stronger than the Moon's. But tides aren't caused by gravity's strength, they're caused by how much it differs across Earth's diameter. That difference falls off as 1/r³ — an inverse cube law. Cube the Sun's much greater distance, and its tidal advantage evaporates: the Moon, despite being 27 million times less massive, wins by more than 2 to 1. See the inverse square law →

Same forcing, different coastline

Both stations peak on the same night. At the June 14 new moon, Kings Point reached 9.17 ft and Montauk Point — facing the open Atlantic 70 miles east — reached 3.23 ft, and got there 2h 11m earlier. Long Island Sound narrows like a funnel toward its western end, so the tidal wave takes time to travel up it and piles up against that geometry when it arrives.

The equilibrium model on the previous tab predicts a spring-to-neap range ratio near 2.7×. Over this same month Kings Point runs 1.7× and Montauk 4.3×. Note too that the smallest ranges arrive around Jun 25, four days after the first-quarter Moon — the Moon was near apogee.
