Two sorting events, one physical property, built the Solar System you see today. Explore how temperature and density decided what each world is made of — and how it got organized.

The Big Idea
Density does two jobs in planet formation. First, it decides which materials are dense (metal, rock) versus light (ice, gas) — and only the dense ones can condense close to a young star. Second, once a planet forms, density decides how those materials sort themselves — dense material sinks, light material rises. Same property, two different moments.

Real Jovian planets are even lighter than this. Once a body this far out grows massive enough, its gravity also captures huge envelopes of hydrogen and helium straight from the nebula — gas that never had to condense at all. That's why Saturn's actual bulk density (0.69 g/cm³) is lower than water.

Same starting ingredients, same gravity — the only difference is heat. A small, cold body (an asteroid, a comet) accretes the same rocky-metal mix as Earth but never melts enough to sort it. It stays a uniform chondrite forever. Earth got hot enough to let density win.
