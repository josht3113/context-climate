Eccentricity is the Volume Knob
When eccentricity is near zero, Earth's orbit is nearly circular — it doesn't matter where the solstices fall, because the Sun is equidistant all year. Eccentricity is the amplitude envelope of precession: stronger eccentricity means precession's seasonal timing shifts drive larger insolation swings. Today's low eccentricity quietly mutes that signal.
The 100k Mystery
Ice cores show the dominant glacial cycle is ~100,000 years — matching eccentricity. Yet eccentricity produces the weakest direct insolation forcing of all three Milankovitch cycles. Why a weak driver generates the strongest signal is one of the open questions in paleoclimate science. Nonlinear ice-sheet dynamics and internal climate feedbacks are the leading explanations.
